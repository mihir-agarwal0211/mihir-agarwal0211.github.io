I'm a graduate student at Columbia University (MS in Computer Science, ML track). I get a lot of DMs and emails asking about the admissions process, so I'm starting this series to answer the most common questions as honestly as I can.

---

## Q: What GRE score do you need for Columbia CS?

Columbia does not publicly state a minimum GRE cutoff, but from my experience and talking to peers, a Quant score in the **165–170 range** is competitive. The Verbal and AWA matter less for CS, but don't neglect them completely.

That said, Columbia's MS CS program has become increasingly holistic. A strong GRE alone won't get you in, and a slightly lower score with a stellar SOP, research background, and strong LORs can absolutely work.

---

## Q: How important is research experience?

Very. Especially for the ML/AI track. If you have published papers, even at workshops, that stands out significantly. If you don't have publications, prior industry ML experience or strong projects (especially with real-world impact or GitHub traction) can substitute.

For the general CS MS, research is helpful but not as strictly required.

---

## Q: What should my Statement of Purpose (SOP) focus on?

Three things:

1. **Why Columbia specifically** — be concrete. Mention professors whose work aligns with yours, the SEAS ecosystem, NYC industry proximity, etc.
2. **What you've done** — highlight 1-2 projects or research experiences in depth rather than listing everything.
3. **What you want to do** — connect your past work to a clear post-graduation goal.

Avoid generic statements like "I've always been passionate about technology." Be specific and show self-awareness.

---

## Q: How many LORs does Columbia require?

Three letters of recommendation. Ideally at least two should be from academic supervisors or research mentors. Industry LORs are accepted and valued, especially if they speak to technical depth.
